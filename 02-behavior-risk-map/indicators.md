# Qualitative indicators an analyst can watch for

Each indicator is a **prompt for a question, not a finding**. One indicator alone usually has an innocent explanation. Two or three together, on a critical supplier, deserve a closer look. Decide who looks closer using the [human-in-the-loop table](../06-human-in-the-loop/README.md).

| # | Indicator | Stage | Why it matters | Corroborate with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | One buyer owns a critical supplier relationship for years with no rotation or second reviewer | SOURCE | Lock-in and single point of knowledge; Kobe Steel cited an insular organization with little personnel rotation [S13] | Assignment history, conflict-of-interest declarations |
| Q2 | Due diligence waived or shortened "because we know them" | SOURCE | Trust replacing verification | DD tracker, waiver approvals |
| Q3 | Expedite requests and premium freight spike before quarter-end | DELIVER | Deadline and incentive pressure | Freight spend by week, sales targets calendar |
| Q4 | Risk acceptances signed under deadline pressure (last day, late hour, missing fields) | ENABLE | Acceptance without real analysis | Risk register metadata, approval timestamps |
| Q5 | Supplier scorecards that never change while hard metrics move | SOURCE | Confirmation bias, scoring as ritual | OTIF, defect rates, scorecard history |
| Q6 | Dissent absent from S&OP or supplier-review minutes for many cycles | PLAN | Groupthink or fear of challenge | Minutes, decision logs, attendance |
| Q7 | Escalations softened in language ("issue" becomes "observation") while counts rise | RETURN / any | Risk understated on the way up | Escalation documents, incident counts |
| Q8 | Overrides concentrated in one approver or one period | Any | Authority bypassing control | Override/exception logs |
| Q9 | Remediation deadlines repeatedly extended for the same supplier | SOURCE / MAKE | Sunk cost, avoidance | CAPA history, contract changes |
| Q10 | Contract changes made by side letter or email outside the contract system | SOURCE | Undocumented risk transfer | Contract change history, legal register |
| Q11 | "Approved by [senior name]" used as the only rationale | Any | Authority replacing analysis | Approval comments |
| Q12 | Tier-2 dependencies unknown for critical parts | SOURCE | Hidden concentration; see [two-suppliers-one-factory](https://github.com/Starboy1968/two-suppliers-one-factory) | Supplier surveys, BOM mapping |

## How to use

1. Start from critical suppliers and critical processes, not from people.
2. Record each indicator as an observation with date, source and who observed it.
3. Corroborate with a hard-data source before calling it a risk.
4. Pass to the decision owner with the evidence, not a conclusion about anyone's motives.

For the deeper power-and-behavior catalog (22 indicators across six lenses: language around risk, meeting dynamics, how dissent is received, informal power and networks, the decision trail, and reporting), see [power-behavior-risk-indicators](https://github.com/Starboy1968/power-behavior-risk-indicators/blob/main/02-framework/indicator-catalog.md). Its [observation note template](https://github.com/Starboy1968/power-behavior-risk-indicators/blob/main/05-analyst-toolkit/observation-note-template.md) and [escalation decision guide](https://github.com/Starboy1968/power-behavior-risk-indicators/blob/main/05-analyst-toolkit/escalation-decision-guide.md) work directly with this list. Same evidence ladder: single observation, repeated pattern, corroborated pattern.
